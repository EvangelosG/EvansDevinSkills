# Q3 FY26 raw notes

Unedited notes from the ops review. This is the *input* to the skill: messy,
repetitive, longer than any slide should be. The skill's job is to turn this
plus the CSVs into a deck, not to be pasted onto slides verbatim.

## What went well

- On-time delivery finished the quarter at 96.7% in September, our best month
  ever, and the quarter averaged 95.9% against a 95.0% target. The Midwest hub
  automation went live in July and is the main driver.
- Cost per shipment came down every month except July. Renegotiated linehaul
  rates in May account for roughly two thirds of the improvement; the rest is
  better trailer utilisation.
- NPS climbed from 38 to 43. Verbatims mention delivery predictability far more
  than price, which supports the reliability-first roadmap.

## What did not

- July was a bad month: cost per shipment ticked up to $38.20 and on-time dipped
  to 94.8%. Root cause was the Atlanta sortation outage (three days) plus peak
  seasonal volume we under-forecast by about 8%.
- Claims rate improved but is still concentrated: two customers account for 41%
  of all claims value. Account team is aware; no remediation plan yet.
- We are still doing the monthly ops pack by hand. It takes an analyst roughly a
  day and a half per month and the numbers have been wrong twice this year.

## Risks going into Q4

- Peak season forecast assumes the Atlanta fix holds. No second failure has been
  observed, but we have not load-tested it at peak volume.
- The linehaul rate renegotiation is a 12-month deal expiring next June.
- Hiring freeze means the Midwest automation rollout to two more hubs slips to
  Q1 unless we get an exception.

## Asks

- Approve the exception for two automation engineers.
- Decision on whether to keep the 95% on-time target or raise it to 96%.
- Sign-off on the claims remediation plan by the end of October.
